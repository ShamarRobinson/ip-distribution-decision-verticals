# Tableau story captions

**Story point 1.** Self-published ISBNs rose 38.7% in 2025.

Every new book gets an ID number called an ISBN. The blue area is books people published on their own, and it jumped 38.7% in 2025 while printed-book sales barely moved (up 0.3%). More books are now competing for the same readers, so keeping more money from each sale matters.

Sources: [Bowker via Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20260323/99943-book-output-topped-4-million-in-2025.html) | [Circana BookScan via Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/bookselling/article/99417-print-book-sales-rose-slightly-in-2025.html)

**Story point 2.** KDP nets $6.79 per paperback; IngramSpark nets $3.33.

Think of each $16.99 book as a pizza. The store takes the gray slice, printing takes the yellow slice and fees take the red slice. The blue slice is what the creator keeps: $6.79 on Amazon KDP, but only $3.33 when bookstores take a 55% discount through IngramSpark.

Sources: [IngramSpark 2026 pricing](https://help.ingramspark.com/hc/en-us/articles/41657495062029-Changes-in-Print-Cost-Market-Access-Fee-and-More) | [KDP royalty calculator](https://profitable.app/tools/kdp-royalty-calculator) | [B&N Press royalties](https://help-press.barnesandnoble.com/hc/en-us/articles/5359658688923-Getting-Paid-for-Sales) | [Lulu pricing](https://blog.chapter.pub/lulu-pricing/)

**Story point 3.** Retailer discounts drive most of the cost variance.

We line every option up against Amazon KDP. Bars to the left mean you keep less money per book than on KDP, and each color shows a reason. For IngramSpark, the store's cut explains $2.55 of the $3.47 gap; printing adds $0.60 and fees add $0.32.

Sources: [IngramSpark 2026 pricing](https://help.ingramspark.com/hc/en-us/articles/41657495062029-Changes-in-Print-Cost-Market-Access-Fee-and-More) | [KDP royalty calculator](https://profitable.app/tools/kdp-royalty-calculator)

**Story point 4.** Direct ebook uploads keep the most per sale.

For a $4.99 ebook, a longer bar means more money for the creator. Selling straight to a store like Apple keeps $3.49. Middlemen such as Draft2Digital keep a small piece (35 to 52 cents) in exchange for sending your book to many stores with one upload.

Sources: [ScribeCount platform comparison](https://scribecount.com/tools/platform-comparison) | [PublishDrive vs Draft2Digital](https://publishdrive.com/comparison-guide-publishdrive-vs-draft2digital.html) | [IngramSpark](https://www.ingramspark.com/)

**Story point 5.** KDP breaks even at 158 unit pairs; IngramSpark at 272.

Starting costs are like a hole you have to fill, and every sale adds a scoop of money. KDP fills the hole after 158 sales (one print plus one ebook) and IngramSpark after 272. On the right, the spot where each line crosses zero is where that option has paid for itself; above zero is profit.

Sources: [USCO 2026 fee schedule (Hypebot)](https://www.hypebot.com/2-months-left-before-u-s-copyright-fees-jump-dramatically/) | [ISBN pricing (ScribeCount)](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/publishing-a-book/How-to-Publish-on-IngramSpark) | [PublishDrive plans](https://help.publishdrive.com/how-much-publishdrives-distribution-pricing-plans)

**Story point 6.** Subscriptions win only above 16.6 units a month.

We tried three 'what if' sales levels: slow (10 a month), normal (50) and great (250). Amazon KDP stays on top every time, so the choice does not depend on guessing sales exactly right. A flat monthly fee (PublishDrive) only beats a per-sale cut once you sell about 17 of each format a month.

Sources: [PublishDrive plans](https://help.publishdrive.com/how-much-publishdrives-distribution-pricing-plans) | [KDP royalty calculator](https://profitable.app/tools/kdp-royalty-calculator) | [B&N Press royalties](https://help-press.barnesandnoble.com/hc/en-us/articles/5359658688923-Getting-Paid-for-Sales)

**Story point 7.** Cash arrives two to three months after each sale.

The colored areas show money you have earned. Stores pay two to three months later, so at month 12 about $948 is earned but not yet in the bank. The bars show how long each store makes you wait: B&N about 45 days, IngramSpark about 105 days.

Sources: [B&N Press payments](https://help-press.barnesandnoble.com/hc/en-us/articles/5359658688923-Getting-Paid-for-Sales) | [IngramSpark](https://www.ingramspark.com/) | [Written Word Media 2025 survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results/)

**Story point 8.** Kindle Unlimited payouts vary month to month.

Kindle Unlimited pays authors from one big shared pot each month, split by how many pages people read. The pot changes, so pay per page goes up and down like the weather: from $4.19 to $5.01 per 1,000 pages. Plan for a range, not one exact number.

Sources: [Profitable.app KENP tracker](https://profitable.app/kdp/stats/kenp-rate) | [Written Word Media 2025 survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results/)

**Story point 9.** A $2 price cut needs a 21.4% sales lift to pay off.

Lowering the price means you earn less on each book, so you must sell more books just to stay even. A $2 print price cut needs 21% more sales to pay off. Keep the cheaper price only if a fair test, with about 18,467 shoppers in each group, shows sales jumped more than that.

Sources: [KDP royalty calculator](https://profitable.app/tools/kdp-royalty-calculator) | [Model and method on GitHub](https://github.com/ShamarRobinson/ip-distribution-decision-verticals)
